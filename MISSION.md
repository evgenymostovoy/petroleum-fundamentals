# Mission: Petroleum Engineering Fundamentals

## Why

15 years as a drilling & completion engineer on the NCS (B.Sc. Gubkin, M.Sc.
NTNU) built strong applied judgment, but the theoretical foundations are
patchy and not automatic — so mental effort goes to basics instead of design
decisions, cross-discipline reasoning, and creative contributions. The goal
is to bring the fundamentals of petroleum engineering (fluid mechanics,
thermodynamics, geomechanics, geology, geophysics, petrophysics, reservoir,
drilling, well design, completions) to the level of a strong M.Sc. graduate —
with core numbers, formulas, and causal chains at **instant recall**.

## Success looks like

- Can follow and **challenge** reasoning from geologists, petrophysicists,
  and reservoir engineers *in their own terms*.
- Can explain the "why" behind every standard drilling and completion design
  choice **from first principles, without looking anything up**.
- Colleagues start coming with cross-discipline questions, not only
  operational ones.

## Constraints

- Not a beginner: rebuild theory *underneath* daily practice; peer-to-peer
  tone, no hand-holding on field operations.
- Instant recall is the bar → every lesson must use retrieval practice,
  spacing, and interleaving (storage strength, not fluency).
- Dual-basin context: company runs NCS projects plus two deepwater Brazil
  projects. SI units first (sg EMW, bar, MPa, m), Brazilian field units
  (psi, lb/gal, meters — the 0.1704 system) always shown second — he must
  operate in both systems.
- Ground examples in both basins where natural: NCS clastics/chalk and
  Brazilian pre-salt carbonates, salt drilling, deepwater narrow margins.

## Out of scope

- Operational procedures and field practice (already mastered).
- Midstream/downstream engineering depth — value chain and upstream
  economics only at the "converse intelligently" level, not specialist level.
